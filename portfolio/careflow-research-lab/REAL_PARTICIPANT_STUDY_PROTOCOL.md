# CareFlow Real-Participant Study Protocol

This protocol is for converting CareFlow from a simulated UX-research demonstration into a study with real participants. It does not retroactively convert seeded demo findings into real findings.

## Research question

How might we reduce uncertainty and handoff friction when a time-sensitive medication request moves between care-team roles?

## Participants

Target 6–8 participants across relevant roles when feasible:
- hospice/palliative nurse or comparable clinical workflow user
- care coordinator / patient-services coordinator
- clinical or medication-administration operations user

Use role-relevant experience, not personal medical history, as the screening basis.

## Privacy and consent

- **No PHI** should be requested, entered, recorded, pasted or discussed.
- Use the fictional CareFlow scenario only.
- Explain that the session is an independent portfolio/product study, not BetterRX research and not clinical care.
- Obtain affirmative consent before recording audio/video or screen activity.
- Participants may stop at any time.
- Store study materials using participant IDs such as P01, P02; do not put participant names into analysis notes unless operationally necessary and separately protected.

## Session structure

1. Welcome, purpose, consent and recording choice — 3 minutes.
2. Background/context questions — 7–10 minutes.
3. Think-aloud usability tasks — 20–25 minutes.
4. Concept comparison / preference reasoning — 5–8 minutes.
5. Debrief and closing — 5 minutes.

## Core tasks

1. Find the medication request most at risk of delay.
2. Identify the missing prerequisite/blocker.
3. Determine who owns the next action and route it correctly.
4. Confirm the request is actually ready for fulfillment.

Do not coach the participant toward the answer. Use neutral prompts such as “What are you looking for?” and “What would you expect to happen next?”

## Capture model

For every task, capture:
- participant ID and role
- task outcome: success / partial / fail
- time on task
- wrong turns or recoveries
- confidence 1–5
- **Observation** — what was directly seen/heard
- verbatim quote when useful
- **Interpretation** — researcher inference, kept separate
- severity
- tags
- follow-up question

Never rewrite interpretation as observation.

## Stop / safety conditions

Stop or redirect if a participant begins sharing identifiable patient information, medication records, screenshots from a live clinical system, credentials, or other confidential employer data.

## Synthesis

After sessions:
1. Normalize notes without deleting contradictory evidence.
2. Cluster observations into themes.
3. Maintain traceability from theme → note → participant/task.
4. Mark unsupported themes as preliminary.
5. Record contrary examples and unresolved questions.
6. Separate frequency from severity.
7. Do not let AI-generated summaries introduce evidence not present in source notes.

A finding should normally have at least two independent evidence points. A single-participant issue may still be reported when severe, but it must be labeled as a single observed case.

## Reporting

For every finding include:
- finding statement
- supporting evidence
- participant roles represented
- contrary evidence
- confidence
- severity
- recommendation
- validation still needed

Report actual sample size and role mix. Do not generalize beyond the recruited population.

## What changes in the portfolio

Only after real sessions are completed:
- label the new material “real participant study” and state the date/sample;
- keep the original simulated pilot clearly identified;
- replace simulated metrics only when a real metric is available;
- preserve study limitations;
- never imply BetterRX sponsored, reviewed or participated unless that is actually true.

## Responsible AI use

AI may assist with formatting, clustering suggestions, duplicate-note detection or summary drafting. Human review remains mandatory. AI must not invent quotes, infer participant demographics, convert interpretations into observations, or erase contradictory evidence.
