# Origin and principles

ServiceBridge grew from a multi-year effort to make sense of fragmented records across military
service, complex medical care, disability benefits, education, employment, and accommodations.
Its design reflects a simple reality: a disabled person's communication may become least polished
when the stakes are highest. The tool should restore structure without erasing voice or treating
neurological, cognitive, or physical limitations as a credibility defect.

The project's working principles are:

1. **Protect credibility.** Precision is stronger than embellishment.
2. **Protect provenance.** Every important factual statement should lead back to a source.
3. **Protect boundaries.** Records, reports, inferences, and unknowns are different things.
4. **Protect privacy.** Use the minimum necessary information for the specific task.
5. **Protect the claimant's position.** External communications should not volunteer speculative
   adverse theories or irrelevant concessions.
6. **Protect autonomy.** The system assists; the person decides.
7. **Respect disability.** Fragmented language during a flare is a communication-access issue, not
   a moral or intellectual failure.
8. **Use the right legal lane.** VA, Social Security, state benefits, education debt, and
   accommodations are not interchangeable.

These principles can support many users. No real claimant's medical history is included in the
public repository.
